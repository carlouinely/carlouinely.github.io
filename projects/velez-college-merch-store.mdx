## Merch sold by hand

Merchandise for the Velez College Supreme Student Council sold by hand, not
through a storefront. Orders came in through messages, availability was tracked
on paper, and buying something on campus meant finding the right person at the
right time.

I built and launched an e-commerce platform for the council so the merchandise
had a sales channel of its own.

## A storefront the council could run

The store lists the council's products with their variants, takes orders, and
records them in one place instead of a chat thread. Students buy on campus
faster, and the council spends its time on the merchandise instead of reconciling
who ordered what.

## Choosing a stack students could maintain

The council is made up of students, and the store has to outlive whoever builds
it. WordPress and WooCommerce were the deliberate call: a well-known storefront
with an editing interface the council already understands, so products and pages
change without a developer.

The store is live at [vcsscmerch.com](https://vcsscmerch.com/).

## The outcome

A manual sales process became a storefront with its own catalog and checkout.
Buying on campus got faster, and the council does less of the work behind each
order.
