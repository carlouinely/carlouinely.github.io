## Merch sold by hand

Merchandise for the Velez College Supreme Student Council was sold through
manual handling rather than a storefront. Orders arrived through messages,
availability was tracked by hand, and buying something on campus meant finding
the right person at the right time.

I built and launched an e-commerce platform for the council so the merchandise
had a dedicated sales channel.

## A storefront the council could run

The store lists the council's products with their variants, takes orders, and
records them in one place instead of a chat thread. Students can buy on campus
faster, and the council spends its time on the merchandise rather than on
reconciling who ordered what.

## Choosing a stack students could maintain

The council is made up of students, and the store has to outlive whoever builds
it. WordPress and WooCommerce were the deliberate choice: a well-known storefront
with an editing interface the council already understands, so products and pages
change without a developer.

The store is live at [vcsscmerch.com](https://vcsscmerch.com/).

## The outcome

A manual sales process became a storefront with its own catalog and checkout.
Purchasing on campus got faster, and the council handles less of the work behind
each order.
