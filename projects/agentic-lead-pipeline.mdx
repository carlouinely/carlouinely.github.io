## Cold outreach starts with the list

An outreach list is only as good as the prospects on it, and a generic list
produces generic email. The first message is usually spent convincing someone
that the sender looked at their site at all.

This internal pipeline at **Zaigo** was built so that first message is specific,
and so the research behind it happens without anyone sitting down to do it.

## Scoring a website instead of guessing

The pipeline sources prospects and then reads their websites, scoring each one on
how well it is doing the job its owner needs it to do. A site that already works
is a poor fit for a rebuild; a low score identifies a business whose web presence
is holding it back.

That score is the decision point. Prospects below the threshold continue down the
pipeline, and everyone else is filtered out before any message is written.

## The mockup is the first contact

For a low-scoring prospect, the pipeline generates a custom mockup of what their
site could be and emails it to them. Instead of a template claiming their site
"could be better", the first contact shows a version of the work.

Sourcing, scoring, and sending run on their own, so a list is worked through
while the people behind it do something else. The implementation is TypeScript,
with language models doing the reading and the mockup generation.

## The outcome

Outreach starts from evidence about each prospect's actual site rather than a
guess from their industry. The first email carries something the recipient can
look at, and the manual research and message writing that used to precede it is a
pipeline that runs continuously.
